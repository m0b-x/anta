# The alarm page on the settings page's own ring. Seeded from
# tool/qa/fixtures/alerts.json, though this flow needs none of its events:
# from the root to the calendar, its ⋮, Calendar settings and the last row of
# the last section — "Test alarm in 10 s" — then the page, read by its ids,
# and Stop.
# - Run it straight after the relaunch that pushed the seed. The fixture's
#   first alarm rings between two and three minutes after the seed was
#   resolved, and a ring that arrives while another is going is queued or
#   dropped by the plugin; this flow takes about twenty seconds.
# - Push the seed with `qa relaunch`, never with `qa run`. `run` resolves the
#   fixture and then builds, so the build comes out of that lead: the first
#   alarm rings inside this flow or, after a longer build, is already in the
#   past when the app imports it, where nothing is armed and 01_event_alarm
#   has nothing to wait for. After a lib change it is two commands: a plain
#   `qa run`, then `qa relaunch --fresh --seed tool/qa/fixtures/alerts.json`.
# - The ring comes ten seconds after the tap (kAlertTestAlarmDelay) and a
#   wait polls for ten, so the wait for the page is given thirty. If it runs
#   out with "This can't ring until a permission is turned on" on screen, the
#   install has lost its notification or exact-alarm grant: a QA build never
#   raises the launch prompt, so grant it by hand once.
# - A test alarm is nobody's event: Open event is there, switched off, and
#   Keep is never offered — three of the page's four ids, the fourth absent.
# - The page calls it "Test alarm", in a row of its own that reads exactly
#   that; "Test alarm in 10 s" is the settings button under the page, and
#   until the redraw of 2026-10 was what the ringing page was titled too.
# - id:alarm-body is the part of the page that scrolls: on a short screen
#   the time leaves through its top, and `scroll-to --up --in id:alarm-body`
#   brings it back.
# - ANTA is in front on an unlocked phone, so Android also draws the ring's
#   notification as a heads-up over the top of the page, a second or two
#   after the page itself; and the page's own bar style — dark status icons
#   on its light ground, where Calendar settings had light ones — reaches
#   the system a moment after its first frame. The shot is adb's, not the
#   agent's, so it shows whatever has arrived by then: `key back` is given
#   three and a half seconds before it, and they are for both. Back itself
#   must do nothing: back is not Stop. The page keeps everything low, so the
#   heads-up in the shot lands on bare ground.
# - Stop pops the page onto Calendar settings; back from there is the
#   calendar, where the next flow starts.
tap "Open navigation menu"
wait id:drawer-calendar
tap id:drawer-calendar
wait id:calendar-add-event
tap id:calendar-more
wait id:calendar-settings-open
tap id:calendar-settings-open
wait id:calendar-add-event --gone
scroll-to id:alerts-test-alarm --max 14
tap id:alerts-test-alarm
wait id:alarm-stop --timeout 30
expect id:alarm-stop id:alarm-snooze id:alarm-open-event id:alarm-body "Test alarm" --absent id:alarm-keep-event --absent "Test alarm in 10 s" --absent id:alarm-open-database
key back --settle 3500
shot 00_test_alarm
tap id:alarm-stop
wait id:alarm-stop --gone
expect id:alerts-test-alarm
key back
wait id:calendar-add-event
errors
